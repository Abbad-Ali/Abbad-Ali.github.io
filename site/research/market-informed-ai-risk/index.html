Work in progress

Market-informed approaches to AI risk

Using market prices and firm activity to estimate the probability of large, non-catastrophic losses under an explicit homogeneity assumption.

Updated October 2026

  • AI safety
  • Economics
  • Policy

Collaborators

Abbad Ali, Stephen H. Shore

My contribution

Economic modeling and quantitative analysis.

Materials

  • Research overview · Stephen H. Shore ↗

I’m working with Professor Stephen H. Shore on how market prices can help identify risky AI activities and the firms engaged in them.

The problem

New AI risks have little historical data. Prediction markets also face a settlement problem: a contract on a catastrophe cannot provide a credible signal if the institutions needed to settle it do not survive.

The approach

We propose feasible contracts for large but non-catastrophic losses, where settlement remains possible. The framework combines market information about which activities are likely to lead to losses with information about which firms engage in those activities.

Under an explicit homogeneity assumption, it yields a firm-specific probability of non-catastrophic loss. This provides a basis for linking remediation and financial penalties to the risks firms impose on society. It does not directly estimate the probability of an existential catastrophe.

Current status

The project is listed on Professor Shore’s research page as Market-Informed Macroprudential Identification and Regulation of Existential Risk from AI.
